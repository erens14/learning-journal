# Phishing Resistance: Protecting the Keys to Your Kingdom

**Source:** NIST Cybersecurity Insights  
**Author:** Andrew Regenscheid and Ryan Galluzzo  
**Link:** [Phishing Resistance – Protecting the Keys to Your Kingdom](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/phishing-resistance-protecting-keys-your-kingdom)  

---

## Summary

This article explains why phishing continues to be one of the most effective cyberattack methods and introduces the concept of **phishing-resistant authentication** as a stronger defense.

Traditional authentication methods, including passwords and SMS-based OTPs, remain vulnerable to phishing and credential theft. NIST recommends adopting authentication mechanisms based on **cryptographic verification**, allowing authenticators to verify the legitimacy of a website before releasing authentication credentials.

The article highlights technologies such as **FIDO/WebAuthn**, **Passkeys**, and **PIV (Personal Identity Verification)** cards as practical implementations of phishing-resistant authentication. These methods prevent credentials from being exposed to fake websites and significantly reduce the risk of credential theft.

Finally, NIST emphasizes that phishing-resistant authentication should be implemented as part of a **layered security strategy**, alongside security awareness training, email protection, network security, and other defensive controls.

## Key Takeaways

MFA is a broad category: passwords plus an SMS OTP can still be phished. The article identifies FIDO/WebAuthn, passkeys, and PIV as cryptographic approaches to phishing resistance.

## Lesson Learned

Evaluate the authentication mechanism's phishing resistance as part of layered defense. Awareness training, email protection, and network controls still have roles outside that authentication boundary.
